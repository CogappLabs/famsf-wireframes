HotJar Website Survey Synthesis

# **Context**

This survey is part of the Discovery phase of FAMSF's 2026 Collections Project - a strategic initiative to modernize how the museum's digital collection is managed, maintained, and experienced by the public. As part of the discovery process, we will post an online survey to collections specific areas of the website to gather visitor feedback on the current experience.

# **Methodology**

* **Format:** Online digital survey
* **Location:** [famsf.org/collections](http://famsf.org/collections) + [famsf.org/art-finder](https://www.famsf.org/art-finder)
* **Audience:** General audience, Researchers
* **Dates:** April 13 - Mary 13, 2026
* **Draft deadline:** April 8, 2026

**Key learning goals for survey:**

* What problems do visitors have with the current collections website?
* What functionality or features matter most to visitors?
* What problems or frustrations exist today that we have an opportunity to solve?
* What does the current website do well?

## User experience

### Search experience

The majority of respondents found what they were looking for, but 19% encountered friction during their search. One respondent was unable to find or was still looking for something at the time of survey completion.

| **Search outcome** | **Count** | **% of total** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yes, easily | 12 | 75% |
| Yes, but it took some effort | 3 | 19% |
| No / still looking | 1 | 6% |

### What went well

Respondents highlighted several strengths of the current site: ease of search, breadth of the collection, museum prominence and credibility in Google results, concise artwork/object descriptions, the metadata table (dates, dimensions, medium, provenance), and exhibition descriptions.

**Primary barrier**

However, respondents surfaced that lack of image quality/size was a barrier. One respondent specifically noted wanting more sculptures as well.

### Search satisfaction ratings

*1 = Very frustrating → 5 = Excellent / easy to use*

While a majority indicated that the search/filters were excellent and easy to use, the remainder of respondents were split - two rating the experience as very frustrating and the remaining six split between a neutral and good experience. This averaged out of 3.75 / 5.

## Feature priorities

Respondents selected features they would find most valuable (requested respondents select 3 options though two selected 4 options and one selected five).

| **Feature** | **Votes** | **% of respondents** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enhanced image tools (deep zoom, hi-res downloads, multiple views) | 12 | 75% |
| Deep content & context (videos, essays, exhibition history on artwork pages) | 9 | 56% |
| Enhanced search (advanced filters: culture, medium, geography) | 8 | 50% |
| Real-time on-view info (object location + currently on view status) | 7 | 44% |
| Personalization (save, organize, and share favorites) | 5 | 31% |
| Academic & research tools (one-click citations, stable URLs) | 5 | 31% |
| Expanded artist info (more comprehensive biographies) | 5 | 31% |
| Expanded collection info (more comprehensive collection area histories) | 5 | 31% |
| Mobile-optimized experience | 1 | 6% |

## Strategic opportunities

* **Image quality as a core experience:** Image quality is simultaneously the biggest frustration and the most-requested feature (75% of respondents). Prioritizing deep zoom, high-resolution downloads, and multiple views of each object addresses the top pain point and delivers the top desired feature in a single investment.
* **Artwork detail pages as information hubs:** 56% of respondents selected deep content and context as high-value. Videos, linked essays, and exhibition history would transform static object records into richer editorial experiences.
* **Improve advanced filters:** 50% of respondents want enhanced filtering and given that finding a specific artwork is a top visitor motivation, reducing the effort required to surface the right result would be extremely beneficial across audience segments.

**Summary**

The survey responses indicate that the collection site is highly valued for its ease of use, comprehensive search functionality, and trustworthy content, especially for research purposes. Users appreciate detailed descriptions and information on exhibitions, but they also highlighted missing sculpture content, inconsistent formats, and incomplete nationality information as barriers. Suggestions for improvement include adding more visual content, expanding donor access, and refining search capabilities.

**Findings**

* Ease of use and robust search features are appreciated by users.
* The site is considered a reliable and trustworthy resource for art research.
* Detailed descriptions and exhibition information support visitor engagement.
* Users noted gaps, such as lack of sculpture coverage and missing images, as main barriers.
* Inconsistencies in data format and classification (e.g., nationality) were cited as obstacles.

**Quotes**

* *"this is a very trustworthy source for research on specific works of art"*
* *"too many without image; would like donor access (Osher, Rockefeller, Hanley etc); 'and not' search tool; inch/cm format inconsistent; tribal names & even European names without nationality; many more!"*
* *"ease of search, large number of artworks"*
* *"Descriptions of upcoming/current exhibitions. I am coming to the Drawn to Venice exhibition and visited the site today to get more background info on the works which will be shown."*
* *"more sculptures"*

**Next steps**

* Add more sculptures to improve content diversity.
* Ensure images are available for all artworks to enhance user experience.
* Implement an 'and not' search tool to refine search capabilities.
* Standardize formatting (e.g., inch/cm and nationality names) across content.
* Consider presenting the survey after site interaction for more informed feedback.
