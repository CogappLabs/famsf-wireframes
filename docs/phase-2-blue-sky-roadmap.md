# FAMSF Collections: Phase 2 / Blue-Sky Roadmap

Working compilation of every Phase 2 ("blue-sky" / North Star) thread, pulled
into one place to drive the fundraising deliverable and the client ideation
workshop.

**This is an internal working doc, not the client deliverable.** The contracted
deliverable is a one-feature-per-slide fundraising deck (see Contract below).
This doc is the raw material, and the place to draft the Phase 2 feature list
that the master Discovery Summary leaves as an empty stub.

## Primary source: the master Discovery Summary

`famsf-discovery-summary-master.md` is now the **canonical synthesis** of all six
discovery activities (stakeholder interviews, Hotjar survey, gap analysis,
landscape audit, UX audit, content audit). Everything below ladders up to it.
Its §"Phase 2: North Star vision" ends with an empty "Feature list". **Filling
that list is the job of this doc.**

The master doc gives us three things to anchor against:

1. **Six audiences** (visit-planners ~67%, art-curious browsers ~37% of survey,
   researchers ~30%, educators, curatorial staff, donors).
2. **Nine strategic opportunities**: Phase 1 covers the foundations
   (opportunities 1–7); Phase 2 is the *richer/interactive/editorial* layer plus
   the ambitious reads of 5, 8, 9.
3. **North Star four pillars** (verbatim from the master doc):
   - A research destination of industry-leading / international standing →
     institutional reputation + scholarly standing
   - A discovery experience that supports all audiences → access + equity
   - A product that reflects FAMSF's civic identity
   - An operational asset → operational sustainability / scalability

## Why Phase 2 exists

- Discovery + Phase 1 spend is capped at **$350k** combined. Phase 2 sits
  outside that and **requires additional fundraising** (FAMSF brief).
- The brief calls Phase 2 cost materials "a **crucial requirement**". They
  exist specifically to support FAMSF's fundraising effort.
- The master doc frames the long-term vision as an online collection that
  "functions as its own primary cultural destination and digital product", past
  technical-debt fixes, into leadership-tier territory.

## Contract: what we owe FAMSF

From the signed SOW (`work-statement-2026.md` §5) and brief
(`famsf-collections-2026-project-brief.md`):

- High-level **Phase 2 roadmap with cost estimates**.
- Summary doc with a dedicated Phase 2 section: **each feature = one slide +
  one abstracted visualization**, written for a **non-technical fundraising
  audience**.
- Time + cost estimates, **per feature or per feature-group** (FAMSF's call on
  grouping).
- Feature prioritization: P0/P1/P2 across Phase 1 vs Phase 2, and an explicit
  statement of **what's excluded from both phases**.

Timeline: Discovery completes **late June '26**, delivering architecture
strategy + North Star vision + costed roadmap. Phase 1 build runs Jul–Dec '26.

## Phase 2 feature list (draft, fills the master-doc stub)

Each entry = a candidate fundraising slide. Tagged with the North Star pillar(s)
it serves, the audience(s) it targets, and the master-doc strategic opportunity
it extends beyond Phase 1. Sources cited for traceability + visuals (mostly the
Landscape Figma).

### A. Onward journeys (guided exploration)

Not "more like this." A way to journey through the collection where the visitor
chooses which rabbithole to follow. Directly answers the master doc's named gap:
art-curious browsers get "no related content or onward journeys, other than
linked artist name."

- **Pillar:** all-audiences discovery · civic identity
- **Audience:** art-curious browsers (~37%), educators
- **Extends:** opportunity 6 (content connectivity) beyond Phase 1's static→dynamic
  recirculation, into authored journeys.
- **Peer anchors:** Rijksmuseum stories-as-entry-points, Getty/Google A&C maker hubs.
- **Source:** Cogapp proposal §Onward journeys; master doc audiences + opp. 6.

### B. Deep artwork engagement (IIIF storytelling)

Guided zoom journey around a single work with expert commentary and specialist
content layered on. Builds on the Phase 1 IIIF server (~70% of collection
already at deep-zoom resolution per master doc).

- **Pillar:** research destination · all-audiences discovery
- **Audience:** researchers, educators, art-curious browsers
- **Extends:** opportunity 2 (images as primary) past Phase 1 deep-zoom into
  authored, annotated storytelling.
- **Peer anchors:** Rijksmuseum "Search Visually," annotated deep-zoom narratives.
- **Source:** Cogapp proposal §Deep artwork engagement; master doc opp. 2.

### C. Front-facing AI: verified + guarded only

The master doc is explicit and **constrains this hard**: openness to AI is
"almost exclusively for backend applications." Front-facing AI raises real
stakeholder concern (Eurocentric bias, hallucination, citation integrity,
curatorial accountability). The *only* sanctioned front-facing exception is a
**verified, publication-fed tool drawing from confirmed FAMSF content**, the
Education team's reading-level adaptation concept.

Phase 2 candidates, all clearly labelled, opt-out for educator contexts,
approved against a sourcing + accountability framework:

- Reading-level adaptation of confirmed FAMSF web text (Education-team concept).
- AI summaries of dense curatorial content, sourced + cited.
- Translation of confirmed content into other languages.

> Do **not** pitch open-ended generative toys (Google A&C Art Remix / Poem
> Postcard style) as FAMSF Phase 2 without flagging the master-doc guardrails.
> They contradict stated stakeholder position. Keep them in a clearly-marked
> "explored, not recommended" appendix if shown at all.

- **Pillar:** all-audiences discovery (access + equity) · research destination
- **Audience:** educators (reading level), researchers (summaries), non-English visitors
- **Extends:** opportunity 8 (AI with guardrails) into the narrow front-facing slice.
- **Source:** master doc learning 7 + opportunity 8; Cogapp proposal §AI interfaces.

### D. Conversion surfaces: tickets + shop

The master doc promotes this to a full strategic opportunity (#5). Object-detail
page as conversion surface: "Get Tickets" CTA on on-view works, related-shop
merchandise links (prints, catalogues, books). Tagged as Enhancements in the
Phase 1 list, natural Phase 2 build-out. High-value: visit-planners ~67% +
art-curious browsers ~37%.

- **Pillar:** operational asset · all-audiences discovery
- **Audience:** visit-planners, art-curious browsers
- **Extends:** opportunity 5 (conversion surface) from Phase 1 Enhancement to full feature.
- **Peer anchors:** Nordstrom adjacent recommendation modules; retail tie-in (gap analysis).
- **Source:** master doc opp. 5; Phase 1 enhancement list; gap-analysis Art-on-Demand signal.

### E. Donor stewardship + civic-identity layer

The master doc elevates this to opportunity 9 with a concrete failure case: the
Anderson family can't find their own gift online. Phase 2 surfaces named
collections + collector/founder stories (Alma Spreckels, M.H. de Young) tied to
San Francisco civic history, with provenance / credit-line / donor-attribution
searchability.

- **Pillar:** civic identity · institutional reputation
- **Audience:** donors, art-curious browsers, researchers
- **Extends:** opportunity 9; named sub-collections (Phase 1 findable) → rich
  collector/founder narrative pages.
- **Peer anchors:** Rijksmuseum community stories; collector-as-entry-point.
- **Source:** master doc learning 2 + opportunity 9; Cogapp proposal §Onward journeys.

### F. Collection-as-product framing

Treat the online collection as broader than an object database: stories,
publications, community contributions as first-class discovery pathways
alongside artworks. Directly answers master-doc learning 2 (the experience
"obscures institutional stature") and learning 9 (existing content unused).

- **Pillar:** civic identity · institutional reputation · all-audiences discovery
- **Audience:** all
- **Extends:** opportunity 6 (content connectivity) into a full editorial product layer.
- **Peer anchors:** Rijksmuseum, AIC, NGA (collection as primary digital product).
- **Source:** master doc learnings 2, 8, 9; landscape synthesis.

### G. Exhibition-history timeline + archival images

Exhibition history timeline with archival images on collection-area / object /
landing pages. **Explicitly named "Blue sky" in the content audit** (object
detail page §Blue sky: "Add archival exhibition images"). Linking out to old
internal exhibition pages is Phase 1-adjacent, but many of those pages are
listings-only (no images / descriptions) per the content audit, the archival
image layer is the Phase 2 ambition.

- **Pillar:** civic identity · research destination
- **Audience:** researchers, art-curious browsers
- **Source:** content audit §Object detail page → Blue sky; `famsf-collection-wireframes/CLAUDE.md` (May '26 FAMSF feedback).

### H. Open-access engagement layer

Engagement metrics (views / downloads) on open-access works, reinforcing the
open-access programme's value beyond a badge. ~7,000 works to web by end of 2026
(master doc) gives this real volume.

- **Pillar:** access + equity · operational asset
- **Audience:** researchers, educators, art-curious browsers
- **Extends:** Phase 1 open-access filtering into a measured engagement surface.
- **Peer anchors:** Unsplash view/download metrics; AIC open access.
- **Source:** master doc learning 1; landscape synthesis (Unsplash analog).

### I. Gamification

Low-barrier hooks for non-engaged visitors. Cogapp built **Artle** for NGA.
What's the FAMSF-unique equivalent? **Caveat:** weigh against master-doc AI/voice
concerns; keep playful tools clearly non-authoritative.

- **Pillar:** all-audiences discovery (access + equity)
- **Audience:** art-curious browsers, educators, general
- **Peer anchors:** NGA Artle, Cleveland tools, Google A&C games.
- **Source:** Cogapp proposal §Gamification; landscape synthesis.

### J. Interactive-tools destination

A single consolidated home for the playful / interactive features (B, C, I) so
they're discoverable in one place rather than scattered.

- **Pillar:** all-audiences discovery
- **Source:** landscape synthesis (Google A&C consolidated interactive section).

### K. Hook / swim / dive content model + dynamic themes

The content audit proposes a **hook → swim → dive** information architecture:
surface visit-planner + art-curious content first (hook), then richer
researcher/educator content below (swim/dive). Pair with **theme-by-tagging**:
themes generated from tags rather than hand-built static pages (current site has
only Environment + Making, can't add more without dev work), made editable +
creatable by curators. Phase 1 makes topic pages dynamic; Phase 2 is the full
tag-driven thematic discovery engine + the hook/swim/dive layout applied across
object, area, and theme pages.

The content audit also flags **label-copy-as-default intro standardisation** as
a "longer-term project", a content-operations workstream worth its own slide
(it improves every object page at once and reduces curatorial burden, master-doc
learning 6).

- **Pillar:** all-audiences discovery (access + equity) · operational asset
- **Audience:** all (hook/swim/dive serves the audience-priority order directly)
- **Extends:** Phase 1 dynamic topic pages → tag-driven theme engine; content
  framework into editorial operations.
- **Source:** content audit §Strategic opportunities (audiences, hook/swim/dive),
  §Theme/topic pages (tagging), §Object detail (label-copy standardisation).

> **Phase-1-vs-2 honesty line (brief requirement):** several items above have
> Phase 1 footholds: advanced search, IIIF zoom, clickable metadata,
> open-access filtering, dynamic recirculation. Phase 2 is the *richer /
> interactive / editorial / conversion* layer on top. The deck must state plainly
> where Phase 1 ends and Phase 2 begins, and what's excluded from both.

## Funder fit: angles by funder archetype

Phase 2 is fundraised separately, so the deck must speak each funder's language,
not just FAMSF's. The same feature gets a different pitch depending on who's
across the table. Existing hooks to build on: the **Sherman Fairchild** grant
already ties funding to demonstrable public-site progress, and the **Open Access
programme** (~7,000 images to web by end 2026) is a live milestone (business-goals
doc). Match features to funders as follows.

### Tech / cloud / AI funders (AWS, Anthropic, Google.org, etc.)

What they fund: showcase deployments of their platform, responsible-AI
exemplars, open infrastructure, reusable patterns other institutions can adopt.

- **Anthropic**: feature **C** (front-facing AI, verified + guarded). The pitch
  is *responsible* museum AI: the master-doc guardrails (no hallucination,
  cited sourcing, curatorial accountability, opt-out for educators) are the
  story, not a constraint. Reading-level adaptation for K–12 = access + equity.
  Frame as a reference implementation for the cultural sector. Cogapp's existing
  Anthropic-prototype work is the credibility anchor.
- **AWS**: features **B** (IIIF deep-zoom storytelling) + **H** (open-access at
  scale) + the data pipeline. The pitch is infrastructure: high-res image
  serving, scalable IIIF, the TMS→web pipeline as an open, reusable architecture.
  AWS funds the "operational asset" pillar: sustainability + scale.
- **Cross-cutting tech angle**: feature **J** (interactive-tools destination)
  and **A** (onward journeys) as demonstrable, sharable digital innovation.
  Whatever is built should be documentable as a pattern peer museums can reuse
  (ties to the Bay Area / sector angle below).

> Keep the AI pitch honest: lead with the guardrails. A funder like Anthropic is
> *more* likely to back the constrained, verified version than an open-ended
> generative toy. The master-doc caution is a selling point here.

### Foundations + grant-makers (Sherman Fairchild, Mellon-style, IMLS, NEH)

What they fund: scholarship, access, preservation, demonstrable institutional
progress, field-wide benefit.

- Feature **B** (deep artwork engagement) + **G** (exhibition history + archival
  images) + **F** (collection-as-product): the *research destination* +
  *institutional reputation* pillars. Scholarly standing, content connectivity,
  surfacing the Achenbach / Rodin / French holdings the master doc says are
  currently invisible.
- Feature **H** (open access) + **K** (content model / access-first
  hook-swim-dive): the *access + equity* pillar. Grant-makers reward open
  access and audience-equity outcomes directly.
- Tie to the **Sherman Fairchild** demonstrable-progress obligation: Phase 2
  deepens what Phase 1 starts.

### Civic / Bay Area / community funders (local family foundations, civic donors, library/education partners)

What they fund: local identity, community benefit, education, equity of access
for Bay Area residents.

- Feature **E** (donor stewardship + civic identity): Spreckels / de Young /
  San Francisco civic history. The collection as part of the city's story. This
  is the most natural Bay Area pitch and ties to named-donor stewardship.
- Feature **C** reading-level adaptation + **K** + educational-resources
  surfacing: direct benefit to **Bay Area K–12 educators and students** (master-doc
  educator audience). Strong community-impact narrative.
- **Other Bay Area institutions** angle: position the open pipeline + IIIF +
  reusable patterns (AWS angle above) as something FAMSF can share with peer
  Bay Area collections (smaller museums, libraries, university collections) that
  can't build it alone. "FAMSF as anchor institution for regional cultural
  digital infrastructure." This doubles as a tech-funder story (reusable, open)
  and a civic story (lifting the whole region).

### Individual / major donors

What they fund: visible, named, legacy-making outcomes tied to relationship.

- Feature **E** (their gift findable + a named collection page): the Anderson
  failure case flipped into the win: "your gift, beautifully presented, findable,
  linkable, told as a story." Highest-leverage 1:1 stewardship pitch.
- Feature **B** / **G** on works tied to a donor's gift: deep engagement +
  exhibition legacy around the objects they care about.

## Workshop ideas (client ideation sessions)

The proposal and discovery-prep doc both name **ideation workshops** as a
Discovery activity, goal: "secure Phase 2 funding." Run these against the
master doc's audiences, opportunities, and four North Star pillars.

### Workshop A: North Star pillar deep-dive

- **Goal:** Pressure-test + enrich the four master-doc pillars (research
  destination / all-audiences / civic identity / operational asset). Agree which
  pillar each Phase 2 feature serves.
- **Who:** Cross-functional FAMSF (curatorial, digital, education, development).
- **Format:** One pillar per table; "what would world-leading look like here?"
- **Output:** Pillar statements the costed roadmap ladders up to.

### Workshop B: Feature dot-voting + prioritization

- **Goal:** Sort features A–K into P0/P1/P2 and Phase 1-vs-2.
- **Format:** Feature wall (one card per A–K), dot-vote impact + excitement,
  place on Now / Next / Later.
- **Output:** Prioritized backlog feeding the brief's P0/P1/P2 requirement + the
  explicit "won't do" list.

### Workshop C: Audience-journey mapping

- **Goal:** Use the six master-doc audiences as lenses. For each, walk the
  collection journey and spot where Phase 2 features unlock the "what they need
  most" / fix the "where the site fails them" columns.
- **Format:** Six persona stations (visit-planner, browser, researcher, educator,
  curatorial, donor); map features onto each journey.
- **Output:** Per-audience evidence that strengthens fundraising narratives.

### Workshop D: Peer landscape reactions

- **Goal:** Use the Landscape Figma (10 peers + 5 cross-industry) as provocation.
  Which peer moves excite FAMSF? Which don't fit?
- **Format:** Gallery walk of annotated peer screens (Rijksmuseum stories, NGA
  Artle, Google A&C tools, Cleveland AI, V&A shuffle, Unsplash metrics).
  Steal-or-skip per screen.
- **Output:** Validated shortlist grounded in real peer precedent.

### Workshop E: AI guardrails alignment

- **Goal:** Given strong stakeholder concern (master doc learning 7), agree the
  sourcing + accountability framework for any front-facing AI, and confirm which
  AI sits backend-only.
- **Who:** Include curatorial + education + a data/eng voice.
- **Format:** Sort AI candidates into backend-only / front-facing-with-guardrails
  / not-doing. Define the verification bar.
- **Output:** The framework that lets feature C be pitched safely; protects the
  deck from over-promising AI.

### Workshop F: Fundraising-story framing

- **Goal:** Shape each shortlisted feature into a fundable donor story.
- **Who:** Include FAMSF development / stewardship team (ties to 2026 business
  goals on donor stewardship + the Anderson-gift failure case).
- **Format:** Per feature, capture audience, visitor outcome, institutional KPI it moves,
  rough cost band, which North Star pillar.
- **Output:** The per-feature narrative + abstracted visualization the SOW
  requires (one slide each, non-technical).

### Workshop G: Content vision framework

- **Goal:** Define the Phase 2 Content Vision Framework (already a named output
  of the High-Level Content Strategy Review).
- **Owner overlap:** Magnolia Molcan (FAMSF), Stephanie supporting.
- **Output:** Content framework scaling Phase 1 requirements into the Phase 2
  editorial/story vision (features A, F, K).

### Workshop H: Funder-narrative mapping

- **Goal:** Route each shortlisted feature to the right funder archetype and
  draft the pitch in that funder's language (see Funder fit above).
- **Who:** FAMSF development / institutional-giving team + a Cogapp tech voice
  (for the AWS / Anthropic / open-infrastructure framing).
- **Format:** Use the funder-fit matrix. Per funder column (Anthropic, AWS,
  foundations, Bay Area/civic, major donors), pick the lead features and write a
  one-paragraph pitch + the institutional outcome that funder cares about. Stress-
  test the Bay Area "anchor institution / regional cultural infrastructure" angle.
- **Output:** Funder-routed pitch variants per feature, feeding grant
  applications + donor conversations (business-goals doc Phase 2 fundraising goal).

### Workshop I: Crazy 8s feature sketching

- **Goal:** Diverge fast on how a shortlisted feature could look or behave, then
  converge to one big-sketch per feature that seeds the SOW deliverable (the
  one-slide abstracted visualisation). Volume over polish.
- **Who:** Mixed FAMSF room (curatorial, digital, education, development); no
  drawing skill required (8 labelled boxes count).
- **Format:** Per round, fold a sheet into 8 panels. Frame one sharp "How might
  we…" pulled from a master-doc named gap (e.g. "HMW make a donor find and feel
  proud of their gift?" for feature E; "HMW give a browser an onward journey
  past the artist link?" for feature A). 8 minutes, 8 sketches, 1 per minute,
  silent and solo. Then self-edit to the strongest one, redraw bigger on a
  solution sheet, gallery walk, dot-vote heat-map, decider picks.
- **Two framings:**
  - *Feature-deepening:* fix one feature card (A–K), sketch 8 variations of it.
  - *Audience-lens:* fix one of the six master-doc audiences, sketch 8 things
    that audience wishes the site did (surfaces gaps the A–K list may miss).
- **Inputs as provocation:** the feature card + the relevant Landscape Figma peer
  anchors (Rijksmuseum stories, NGA Artle, Google A&C tools).
- **Guardrail:** for feature **C**, run the round *inside* the AI guardrails
  (HMW = verified, cited, opt-out for educators) so sketches don't drift into the
  generative-toy territory the master doc warns against.
- **Output:** One chosen big-sketch per shortlisted feature, feeding the
  per-feature slide + abstracted visualisation the SOW requires. Pairs naturally
  with Workshop B dot-voting (sketch first, then prioritise).
- **Watch-outs:** only run on the 3–4 post-vote shortlist (not all 11, or sketches
  go shallow); remote = FigJam frame with on-screen timer.
