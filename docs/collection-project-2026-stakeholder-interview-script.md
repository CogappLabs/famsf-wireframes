# Internal Context

This interview is part of the Discovery phase of FAMSF's 2026 Collections Project - a strategic initiative to modernize how the museum's digital collection is managed, maintained, and experienced by the public.

* **Interview format:** Small group sessions, 45-60mins
* **Interview dates:** March 23 - April 3, 2026 (tentative)
* **Interview scheduling deadline:** Friday, March 20, 2026
* **Script deadline:** Week of March 16, 2026
* **Output:** Findings report, aspirational institution examples, initial feature wishlist

**Key learning goals for interviews:**

* What problems do curators, researchers, or educators have with the current collections website?
* What functionality or features matter most to you and the people you serve?
* What is hardest to maintain today?
* What problems or frustrations exist today that we have an opportunity to solve?
* What functionality must never break?
* Which peer institutions do you admire as a digital north star?
* What frustrates users most often?
* What would success look like for this project?
* What operational or engineering pain points slow the team down?
* What opportunities are we currently missing?

# Opening (5mins)

Thank you for joining us. I’m Stephanie Amaro. Also here to listen in are [insert names].

For context, we are in the early discovery phase of the 2026 Collections Project. This project acts as a major effort to modernize our digital infrastructure and define a new long-term vision for how our collections are experienced online in the next three years.

We have two major phases planned:

* Phase 1 which will focus on improving back-end infrastructure, de-coupling collections data from our main website database, and introducing a few “must have” improvements to the front-end (think search, metadata).
* Phase 2 defines a 3-year north star for how our collections are experienced online, which will support our fundraising strategy

As a key stakeholder group, your perspective is foundational to helping us define scope for both phases of work. As such, we will spend the next 45 - 60 mins talking through current experience of the collections site, thinking through what “success” might look like, and walking through your provided examples of inspirational digital collection experiences.

I’d like to record this so we can stay present in the discussion—is everyone comfortable with that? Any questions before we dive in?

Great, to kickoff, can you each say your name, your role, and roughly how often you interact with the collections website in a typical week?

# Section 1: Current Reality + Audiences (10mins)

**Goal:** Identify who uses the site today and where it fails them.

* **Workflow:** Walk me through a recent time you (or a researcher/student you work with) used the collections site. What were you trying to achieve?
  + Did you find what you needed? If not, what was the 'dead end'?
* **Audience gap:** Who is using the site successfully today? More importantly, who is NOT using it that you wish was? (e.g., specific scholars, local students, global curators).
* **Workarounds**: When the website fails, what is the 'manual' workaround? (e.g., direct email, third-party site).
  + What internal tasks would disappear if the site worked perfectly?

# Section 2: Technical Friction (10mins)

**Goal:** Surface pain points and critical features.

* **Maintenance burden:** For those of you managing data—what feels 'harder than it should be'? (e.g., image rights, updating metadata, linking related objects, knowing what will and will not be pushed to the website).
* **Functionality:** If you came in on Monday and one thing on the collections site was broken, what would cause the most chaos for your work?
* **eMuseums:** If you came in on Monday and eMuseum was decommissioned, how would this impact your workflow? What functionality would need to be replicated elsewhere? Are there any other concerns we should be aware of related to eMuseum?
* **Missing metadata:** Are there specific fields or filters (geography, provenance, exhibition history) that are 'invisible' or hard to search today?
