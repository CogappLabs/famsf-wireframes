# Content Audit \+ Recommendations Summary

# Context

The 2026 Collections Project is a strategic initiative to modernize how FAMSF's digital collection is managed and experienced online. This content strategy work will be conducted to ensure that content requirements are incorporated into scoping for Phase 1 and that a clear content framework is in place to guide the Phase 2 vision.

## **Goal**

Audit how collection content is currently structured and presented on the FAMSF website, and define content recommendations to inform scoping for both phases of the 2026 Collections Project.

## **Scope**

This audit covers the collections section of the FAMSF website across five areas: collection landing and department pages (hierarchy, navigation, intro content); object detail pages (fields displayed, gaps, and inconsistencies); Artfinder search and filters (architecture, nomenclature, missing filter types); editorial cross-linking (where stories, audio, and other content currently connect to collection pages); and content debt (broken, orphaned, or inconsistently maintained content).

This work will contribute to answering these questions:

* Which object detail page fields are required for Phase 1, optional for Phase 1, and aspirational for Phase 2?  
* How should collection narrative content — department introductions, thematic stories, educational context — be structured in the new site?  
* How does content need to serve different audiences: art explorers, researchers, and educators?

# Key findings

* **Collection landing and department pages**  
  * Collection artworks are currently organized loosely by department/collecting area, with some departments broken into additional collecting areas (e.g., European paintings \+ decorative arts is listed as European decorative art, European paintings, and European sculpture art; Achenbach is listed as Works on paper and Photography). This was intended to make them more navigable.

      * Costume \+ textile arts  
  * Named sub-collections, such as the Anderson Graphic Arts Collection, fall under larger collecting areas, and aren’t searchable; they can only be found with a direct link.  
  * The landing page has limited ways art can be viewed/explored: Highlights, Collection, or Topic (currently only Environment and Making). These are largely static, and have to be manually updated.  
  * Collecting areas currently have their own landing pages, including Highlights and related Stories, with “About” descriptions as sub-pages.  
* **Search and filters**  
  * Our advanced search is closer to a basic search: What, Who, When, Collection.  
  * Filters are not well-organized (not alphabetical), most can’t be searched, and some give multiple selections that look identical, such as multiple “anonymous” artists in the Who field).  
  * Current Collections search landing page, pre-search, appears to have artworks listed in reverse alphabetical order, with no prioritization.  
* **Editorial cross-linking**  
  * Currently, the only cross-linking is a limited number of audio items on object detail pages and stories on topic pages/topic displays on the landing page.  
* **Content debt**  
  * The topic pages are largely static. Story content updates, but artworks don’t. There are currently only two pages: making and environment. The others were retired and you can’t create new pages.  
  * Many object pages are missing an introductory description. Provenance, bibliography, and exhibition history are newer fields, and aren’t consistently available.  
  * Many old exhibition pages are listings only, without images or a description, to consider if we want to link to the pages from object pages.  
* **Audiences**  
  * The current site is largely designed with visit-planners and art-curious browsers in mind. This is reflected in the prioritization of artwork highlights and related editorial content above information on departments/collecting areas, as well as very limited search functionality.  
  * Object pages are largely designed for researchers, but a lack of exposed fields means they are not serving that audience well.

# Strategic opportunities

* **Artwork discovery**   
  * Increase discovery of collection artworks by revising/expanding how they are organized and searched. Moving away from organizing by department, or introducing more ways to categorize the art, would greatly increase navigability.  
  * Increase artwork exploration by making fields on object detail pages clickable, and suggest related content (e.g., related, same time and place, in the same medium, in the same gallery).  
* **Search**  
  * Significantly expand what existing fields are searchable, and introduce more intuitive ways to search (such as periods and styles, highlights, open access, popular).  
  * Improve usability of filters, making order more intuitive and making all filters searchable.  
  * Improve and expand options for ordering artworks (pre- and post-search).  
* **Rich content**  
  * Build on current site’s structure as a content hub by adding additional related content to object pages (audio, articles, educational content, catalogue essays, etc.)   
* **Audiences**  
  * Better serve multiple audiences by integrating additional information/fields on object pages and within search.  
  * Expanding on current experience, organize information into a hook, swim, dive format, where information serving visit-planners and art-curious browsers is surfaced first, followed by richer/deeper dive content for researchers, educators, and others.

# Recommendations summary

## **Home page**

* Update from “Explore our collections” to a “Highlights” module higher on the page  
* Update button to “Search \[or Explore\] our collections”

## **Our collections \[collections landing page\]**

* Add basic search filters, such as: Highlights, Open access, Popular, or move current “Who, What, Where, etc. ” filters from advanced to basic.  
* Add more entry points beyond current highlights, collection, topic (need to expand topic if keeping)  
* Review/update intro copy  
* Fix image sizing

## **Collections search**

* Rework/expand what TMS fields we're pulling from (ex: multiple "anonymous" for "Who")  
* Better organize filter order (not alpha/clear order)  
* Add: periods and styles, metadata fields like credit line and object number; highlights, open access, popular  
* Add ability to set own time range  
* Make all filters searchable  
* Have different results sorting options \[Met has: Relevance, title A-Z, title Z-A, Date, artist/maker, Accession number \[all forward and back\]  
* Potentially add additional intro text (besides search box)

## **Object detail page**

* Build out related content \[audio, articles, educational content, etc.\]  
* Better standardize intro text; suggest using label copy where possible \[longer-term project?\]  
* Adding catalogue entry or other deeper dive academic content lower on page  
* Links to related content: make other fields clickable; suggest related: related, same time and place, in the same medium, in the same gallery?  
* Images: add zoom, open access/download, share, multiple views  
* Adjust formatting: sentence case for sections; italicize titles (vs. descriptions of objects); richer/easier formatting in description and lower sections like provenance, including italics, curly apostrophes, and para breaks  
* On view: include gallery subject not just number; match to Bloomberg  
* Add formatted, run it caption/credit for easy citation  
* Link to previous internal exhibition pages. Note: many old exhibition pages are incomplete (no images, descriptions, etc.)

### Blue sky

* Add archival exhibition images

## **Collecting area pages / about pages**

* Review/refresh intro line; consider simplifying to remove “This collection” (ex: “This collections features historic and contemporary art from...” to “Historic and contemporary art from...”  
* Review/refresh about copy  
* Standardize number of highlights, if keeping structure similar  
* Fix image sizing  
* Link on landing page says "About the XX collection" but page says “About XX.” Make consistent.  
* Fix capitalization to make sentence case where not a proper noun (Currently: About Ancient art, About Costume \+ textile arts).  
* Keep exploring is static (always lists the same three collections). Revise recirculation.  
* Evaluate placement of about copy (sub-page, expanded copy, sep section, or separating art highlights completely from about, if no longer having departments as main division.  
* Also consider a short, general audience description emphasizing scope, areas of strength, and highlights, with secondary sections on history of collection, donors, etc.

## **Theme/topic pages**

* Expand number of themes (or create with tagging, rather than as pages)  
* Make themes editable  
* Add ability to create new themes  
* Change how artworks are identified, so not static
