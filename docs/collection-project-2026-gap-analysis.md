# **Project context**

This report consolidates findings from two distinct research initiatives to inform the 2026 Collections project: the Frankly Green + Webb online audience research (2024) and the Web2.0 Discovery internal stakeholder survey (2025). Together, these sources highlight persistent gaps between the museum's digital collection offerings and the expectations of researchers, curators, educators, and general visitors. The focus is on the Researcher audience experience, curatorial staff input, and findings directly related to artwork and collections website content.

**Research context:**

* **Frankly Greene + Webb (2024)** was commissioned following the 2021–2022 website redesign to assess how audience experience had evolved and identify opportunities for improvement. It utilized website surveys, Hotjar content engagement analysis, and usability testing and interviews. Researchers were defined as professional or academic users of museum websites (art historians, curators, scholars, professors, conservators, graduate students, librarians, and archivists).
* **Web2.0 Discovery (2025)** was conducted as part of a broader initiative to enhance the FAMSF website, focused on usability and visitor experience. Ten participants from the Curatorial department completed a survey with a focus on the Collections area of the site.

**Key question:** What problems surfaced during the 2024 research and Web2.0 discovery still exist, and which requirements were deferred to the Collections project scope?

# **Major theme**

Both work highlighted that there is a significant gap between what the website currently does and what visitors expect it to do in order to meet their needs.

The 2022 redesign was visually successful and created strong brand cohesion between both museums but made advanced features invisible to visitors. Curatorial departments reported that the site fails as a primary research, discovery, or educational tool - content is fragmented, search is unreliable, and key metadata remains inaccessible.

# **Audience + motivations**

* **General audiences** make up over two-thirds of all website users and are primarily planning trips, purchasing tickets, or checking what's on view. A recurring pain point is confusion about which museum houses specific collections, and users struggle to distinguish "On View" status.
* **Researchers** represent approximately 8–10% of total users but are highly active and grew by 3% in 2023. They use the website to search for specific artworks, subject areas, or to assess exhibitions for visit planning. They achieve an 80% task-success rate but are consistently frustrated by search limitations, metadata gaps, and lack of basic image standards (high-resolution imagery and IIIF zoom).
* **Curatorial staff** mostly rely on internal databases (TMS, eMuseum) for collection lookups, bypassing the collections website entirely. They regularly assist external researchers who cannot navigate the online collection which poses a significant and ongoing operational burden.
* **K–12 educators** look for specific resources for field trips and historical context. The Web2.0 project introduced a new "Learn + Engage" header with more intentional subheadings to address their core frustrations of being unable to locate content, though the updates didn’t address deeper integration with Collections content.
* **Retail visitors** are often looking for Art on Demand (custom prints) which illustrates an opportunity to link Collection search more directly to retail conversion.

# **Key pain points**

* **Search + discoverability:** Users frequently request features (filtering by artist, material, or location) that already exist because they are not readily viewable indicating an opportunity to improve the collection search entry point. Curatorial staff describe the collections search as "unreliable" and often do not use it for their own quick lookups.
* Missing filter parameters include: geography and cultural group, donor name, object type, and date range (year-level minimum).
* **Metadata gaps:** Both studies identified the same categories of missing or incomplete data: culture and geographic origin, provenance and exhibition history, bibliography and scholarly references, and artist biographies. Provenance, exhibition history, and bibliography have since been added.
* **Image quality + download experience:** High-resolution images with deep zoom are considered baseline expectations. The previous download button opened an image in a new browser tab rather than saving it to the user's device, causing confusion. The download feature has since been removed from artwork detail pages entirely, but no pathway to access images has been added as a replacement. Multiple images per artwork detail page are not currently supported, but are desired.
* **Content integration + linking:** Related stories, videos, scholarly essays, and educational content are not linked directly from artwork or exhibition pages. There is no mechanism to link artworks to the past exhibitions in which they appeared.
* **Museum + collection location clarity:** Collection areas are not clearly associated with a museum location. Users, including researchers, struggle to understand which institution houses which collections.

# **Strategic opportunities for the 2026 collections project**

* **Improved search experience.** Structure the Collections landing page around a search-first model that supports both deep research and casual discovery - exposed quick filters for basic search and more robust advanced filters.
* **Metadata + data pipeline expansion.** Automate data pipeline to pull missing metadata from TMS into public-facing collection records, prioritizing metadata such as culture.
* **IIIF Image server implementation.** Implement IIIF to provide high-resolution zoom and establish clear pathways to access images (download or request access).
* **Content integration + contextual linking.** Link related stories, articles, and educational resources directly on artwork detail pages. Bi-directional linking: artwork → past exhibitions it appeared in, and exhibition → all featured artworks.
* **Museum + collection location clarity.** Clearly communicate which collection areas are housed at de Young vs. Legion of Honor to aid planning oriented visitors.
* **Collections as a destination.** Elevate permanent collections by making the collections area a destination with robust curatorial department pages, surface archive access, contact information, and practical visit planning.
* **Retail + conversion integration.** Explore tighter linking between Collection pages (especially artwork detail pages) and specific retail offerings.

## **Major theme**

While the 2022 redesign was visually successful and created a strong brand cohesion between both museums, it created a gap where advanced features exist but are invisible to visitors.

## **Audience profiles + motivations**

* **Visit-focused audiences:** Over 2/3 of users are visit-oriented (planning, ticketing).
* **Growing researcher segment:** While only 10% of the audience, this group is highly active and saw a 3% increase in 2023.

* **Researcher audience**
  + Typically utilizing the website to search for specific artworks and subject areas or seek practical information about exhibitions to assess whether those exhibitions are pertinent to their research/to plan visits

## **The "Art Finder" + search paradox**

There is a major discrepancy between available features and user awareness:

* **Invisible functionality**: Users frequently requested features that already exist (e.g. filtering by artist, material, or location), but they couldn't find the "Art Finder" link or the filter sidebar.
* **Labeling confusion:** The term "Art Finder" is non-intuitive for researchers; many prefer "Advanced Search" or "Search Filters."
  + *Note: Since this research was conducted, “Art Finder” has since been replaced by “Advanced search”*
* **Entry point issues:** Users start searches from the main site search or collections landing page, where filters are currently hidden.

## **Top researcher pain points**

Researchers (8% of users) are successful 80% of the time in finding what they were looking for but are frustrated by the following:

* **Image quality:** A universal demand for High-resolution images and deep zoom functionality (IIIF).
* **Download behavior:** Visitors are confused/surprised that the download button doesn't immediately save the file to their machine.
  + *Note: The download feature has been removed from artwork detail pages since this research was conducted.*
* **Missing metadata:** Researchers cited missing data for:
  + Culture + geographic origin
  + Provenance + exhibition history
  + Bibliography + artist bios
* **Contextual links:** There’s an expressed desire to see stories/articles linked directly on the artwork detail page rather than just on a separate "Stories" landing page to streamline research and provide more robust information offerings.
  + *“I wished there was more about how the artist made the work, or a few links to relevant publications or an artist interview. Conservation research can open a whole new dimension of understanding a culture or time period. It can also inspire and engage artists, scientist students, historians, educators, and more.” - Researcher Survey Respondent*

## **Retail + educator insights**

* **Retail:** 15% of shop visitors are looking for Art on Demand (custom prints).
  + *Note: There is an opportunity to link the Collection search more tightly to retail conversion.*
* **Educators:** Primarily looking for specific resources for K-12 field trips and historical context. They find the current "Art + Stories" grouping confusing and would prefer direct access to educational resources that they can download or use to prepare for their visit.
  + *Note: The Web2.0 project formally introduced a new “Learn + engage” header to the website that houses Educational and Story content with more intentional subheadings.*

## **Strategic opportunities for 2026 Collections project**

* Higher visibility of collection search filters on the Collections homepage and/or main website search
* Data expansion effort to pull in “missing” metadata (Automated data pipeline)
* Implement a IIIF Image Server to provide the high-res zoom functionality that researchers consider a standard requirement.
* Opportunity to better structure content on the main collections landing page to support a “search-first” research experience to emphasize the depth of our archives while also supporting a more casual discovery mindset of general audiences.
  + Holistically review all collections related pages including thematic groupings.
  + Clarify Collection areas and enhance the user’s understanding of what Collections are associated with which museum(s)
  + Emphasize past and present exhibitions in Collections area of site and ways to archive lists of exhibited artworks on exhibition pages
  + Emphasize access to other resources (archives, contact information, practical planning) sought by researchers in Collections area of site

## **Project Scope**

The Web2.0 project was an initiative to enhance the main FAMSF website, focused on solidifying cohesive product strategy, improving usability and accessibility, and upleveling the overall visitor experience. As part of the project, an online survey was conducted wherein 10 participants across the Curatorial department shared direct feedback on the current state of the website with a focus on the Collections area of the website. The following document summarizes these findings as well as initial collection specific v1 requirements that were not included within the scope of the Web2.0 project but should be evaluated for the Collections project scope.

## **Major Theme**

The Web2.0 research revealed that one of the major failures of the current website is to surface the depth and richness of FAMSF's permanent collections to visitors. While the 2022 redesign improved visual cohesion, curatorial departments uniformly report that the website fails as a primary research, discovery, or educational tool – content is fragmented, search is unreliable, and key metadata remains inaccessible.

## **Audiences + Motivations**

### Curatorial Staff (external audience proxy)

* Most rely on internal databases (TMS, eMuseum) for their own collection lookups, bypassing the public website entirely.
* Staff actively assist external researchers who cannot navigate the online collection which results in a significant burden on staff to manually address current failures of the website.

### Researchers

* Seeking specific artworks by geography, culture, donor, or material — parameters not currently searchable.
* Expect high-resolution images and IIIF zoom as baseline functionality.
* Frustrated by missing metadata: provenance, exhibition history, bibliography, geographic origin.

### Educators (K–12 + Institutions)

* Looking for downloadable field trip resources and historical context.
* Want education resources to be better integrated into collections content.

### General Audiences + Visitors

* Primarily visit-oriented (planning, ticketing, what's on view).
* Confused by which collection areas are housed at de Young vs. Legion of Honor.
* Struggle to distinguish "On View" from "In Storage."

## **Key Pain Points by Area**

### Collections Discoverability

* The collections search is described as "unreliable" — curators do not trust it for their own quick lookups.
* No ability to search by geography, cultural group, donor name, or object type.
* Collection areas are not clearly associated with museum location (de Young vs. Legion of Honor).
* Content related to collections (programs, exhibitions, articles) is scattered across unrelated site sections with no links back to collection pages.

*"As a member of a curatorial department that hosts research visits I have noticed that a lot of researchers... have a very hard time using the online collection database to find works... This is most often a problem when researchers want to make a list of objects based on a particular geographic location or cultural group because these parameters are not searchable." — Assistant Curator, Costume & Textile Arts*

### Search Filters + Advanced Search

* Search filters are not visible from the main site search or collections landing page — visitors cannot find them.
* The renaming of "Art Finder" → "Advanced Search" was an improvement but filter visibility remains a P1 issue.
* Date range filtering (at least year-level) is missing.
* Geographic origin and physical location (on view / in storage) filters are not available.

### Metadata Gaps

* Missing or incomplete data for: culture and geographic origin, provenance and exhibition history, bibliography, and artist bios.
* Curators express desire for attribution and citation of article authors on collection content.

### Image Quality + Download Experience

* High-resolution imagery with deep zoom (IIIF) is considered a baseline expectation.
* Image download behavior was confusing: the button opened an image in a new tab rather than saving to the device. (Note: download feature has since been removed from artwork detail pages.)
* Multiple images per artwork detail page are not currently supported but desired.

### Content Integration + Contextual Linking

* Related stories, videos, scholarly essays, and educational content are not linked directly from artwork or exhibition pages.
* Exhibition pages do not archive lists of featured artworks — making past exhibitions non-researchable.
* No mechanism to link artworks to past exhibitions they appeared in.

*"I wished there was more about how the artist made the work, or a few links to relevant publications or an artist interview. Conservation research can open a whole new dimension of understanding a culture or time period."*

## **Strategic opportunities for 2026 Collections project**

1. **Search-first Collections Experience**
   1. Expose advanced search filters prominently on the Collections homepage and main site search.
   2. Add geographic, cultural, donor, and date-range filter parameters.
   3. Structure the collections landing page around a "search-first" model that supports both deep research and casual discovery.
2. **Metadata + Data Pipeline Expansion**
   1. Initiate an automated data pipeline to pull missing metadata from TMS into the public-facing collection records.
   2. Prioritize: culture/geographic origin, provenance, exhibition history, bibliography, and artist bios.
   3. Establish clear content update workflows between curatorial departments and the web team.
3. **IIIF Image Server Implementation**
   1. Implement a IIIF Image Server to provide high-resolution zoom.
   2. Resolve the gap left by the removal of the download feature; offer a clear image access pathway for researchers.
4. **Content Integration + Contextual Linking**
   1. Link related Stories, articles, and educational resources directly on artwork detail pages.
   2. Archive past exhibition pages with complete artwork lists.
   3. Bi-directional linking: artwork → past exhibitions it appeared in, and exhibition → all featured artworks.
5. **Museum + Collection Location Clarity**
   1. Clearly communicate which collection areas are housed at de Young vs. Legion of Honor.
   2. Differentiate "On View" from "In Storage" at the collection and artwork level.
   3. Review all collection-related thematic groupings for clarity and consistency.
6. **Dedicated Collection Hubs**
   1. Build robust, dedicated collection pages per curatorial area with integrated history, highlights, related programs, and digital assets.
   2. Surface archive access, contact information, and practical visit information within the Collections area.
   3. Elevate the permanent collection as a daily destination, not just a supplement to special exhibitions.

## **Themes & Key Takeaways**

The curatorial departments view the website as a crucial interface for sharing the museum's collections and scholarship with the world. They commend its visual presentation of special exhibitions but universally identify significant functional and content gaps that impede their ability to serve their audiences and achieve departmental goals.

**Key Pain Points:**

* **Collection Discoverability:** The most pervasive issue is the difficulty in finding and navigating information about the permanent collections. The search function is unreliable, and content is fragmented, making it challenging for researchers, educators, and general audiences to access in-depth artwork details, related scholarship, or even basic collection highlights.
* **Navigation & Calendar:** Users consistently struggle with the website's navigation, particularly the calendar, which is described as "onerous" and difficult for finding daily events, lectures, and non-exhibition programming.
* **User Experience & Accessibility:** The website is criticized for its "clunky" design, poor mobile responsiveness (due to large images/text leading to excessive scrolling), and general lack of user-friendliness, which directly impacts accessibility for various audiences.
* **Research Support:** Curators frequently resort to internal databases (TMS, eMuseum) for their own work and to assist external researchers, highlighting the website's failure as a primary research tool. Specific needs include geographic search parameters and better access to high-resolution images.
* **Content Management:** There's a perceived lack of clear workflows for content updates and a desire for more dynamic, interconnected content that goes beyond static exhibition pages.

**Overarching Desires:**

* **Integrated Collection Hub:** A strong desire for dedicated, robust, and easily navigable collection pages that serve as a central hub for all related content (history, highlights, programs, research, media).
* **Enhanced Functionality for Specialized Audiences:** A clear need to better serve researchers, educators, and artists with specific search capabilities, accessible scholarly content, and improved event listings.
* **User-Centric Redesign:** A move towards a more intuitive, mobile-friendly, and accessible design that prioritizes information discoverability and a seamless user journey.

## **Departmental Call to Action**

The curatorial departments seek to re-establish the website as a robust, searchable, and integrated platform for sharing the museum's vast collections and scholarly work. Prioritization should be given to revamping collection discoverability, streamlining navigation (especially the calendar), enhancing accessibility, and integrating functionalities that directly support research and deeper engagement with the permanent collection. These improvements are crucial to elevating user satisfaction, increasing online engagement, and effectively achieving the museum's educational and cultural mission.

## **Key Areas for Improvement**

Here are the most critical areas identified for website enhancement:

* **Collection Information & Search Enhancement:** The primary challenge is the website's inability to effectively showcase and allow users to search the permanent collection. This includes implementing robust, dedicated collection pages with contextual information, ensuring accurate and comprehensive data (provenance, bibliography), and drastically improving search functionality to include advanced parameters like geography, donor names, and keyword search, making objects easily discoverable both on-site and via external search engines.
* **Navigation & Calendar Overhaul:** The current navigation is confusing, and the calendar is particularly difficult to use, obscuring daily activities, lectures, and non-exhibition programming. A redesign is needed to provide intuitive pathways to all content, with a special focus on a user-friendly and comprehensive events calendar.
* **User Experience & Mobile Responsiveness:** The website's design is perceived as clunky, with oversized elements hindering mobile usability and requiring excessive scrolling. Improvements are needed to create a streamlined, visually appealing, and highly responsive experience across all devices, ensuring critical information and calls-to-action are easily accessible.
* **Accessibility & Content Accuracy:** Ensuring the website is fully accessible to all users, including those with disabilities, is critical. This includes not just technical compliance but also clear, accurate, and properly credited content, addressing reported inconsistencies and outdated information.
* **Integration for Richer Content:** The departments desire the ability to easily integrate diverse media (zoomable images, videos, audio, related stories, scholarly essays) directly onto artwork and exhibition pages to provide a more immersive and comprehensive online experience. This extends to better integration with internal systems like TMS for seamless content updates.

## **Areas of Focus for Supporting the Curatorial Departments**

To address the identified challenges and empower the curatorial departments, focus should be placed on the following:

* **Develop a Comprehensive Collections Strategy:**
  + Prioritize building out robust, dedicated collection pages for each curatorial area, featuring highlights, related projects, digital assets, and integrated contextual information.
  + Implement advanced search functionalities for the collections database, including parameters like geography, donor names, object titles, and clear attribution details.
  + Streamline content ingestion from TMS to ensure accuracy and timely updates, and explore automation where possible.
* **Redesign Core Navigation and Calendar:**
  + Conduct a thorough UX audit of the website's overall navigation and the calendar specifically, with the goal of creating an intuitive and easy-to-use system.
  + Ensure all daily activities, lectures, and non-exhibition programs are prominently and clearly listed, allowing for easy filtering and discovery.
* **Optimize for User Experience and Accessibility:**
  + Undertake a mobile-first design approach, optimizing image and text sizes to reduce scrolling and improve readability on smaller screens.
  + Ensure all content is accessible and consider multilingual options to serve diverse global audiences.
* **Empower Curators with Content Tools:**
  + Provide tools for curatorial departments to easily embed and manage rich media (zoomable images, videos, audio) directly on relevant pages.
  + Establish clear workflows for content creation, editing, and publishing that minimize inaccuracies and ensure proper attribution.
* **Leverage Digital for Research & Scholarship:**
  + Explore ways to make past exhibition pages and scholarly articles more searchable and discoverable, creating a valuable research archive.
  + Support the integration of academic resources and tools that can benefit researchers using the site.
* **Enhance Revenue Opportunities through Content:**
  + Strategically place "click to buy" links for relevant retail products (e.g., exhibition catalogs) directly on exhibition or collection pages.
  + Explore opportunities for donor visibility or sponsorship on high-traffic content areas related to specific collections or thematic hubs.

### **Department's Needs Met by Website (1-5 Scale)**

The average rating for departmental needs being met was **2.0**, indicating that the website largely *does not* adequately support their work. Key detailed feedback included:

* Strong desire for **dedicated, robust collection pages** with integrated history, highlights, and related digital assets.
* Frustration with scattered content (programs, exhibitions, articles) not being directly linked to collection pages, hindering a cohesive narrative.
* Unreliable collection search and difficulty with the calendar feature.
* Needs for proper content citation and author credit.

### **What the Current Website Does Well**

Common praises for the current website include its ability to **effectively showcase exhibitions** (unanimous), and to a lesser extent, **clearly highlight the FAMSF mission and values**, and **facilitate easy ticket purchases**. Some respondents also noted it offers engaging content and acts as a useful tool for visit planning.

### **Biggest Challenges Facing Website Users**

Overwhelmingly common challenges include:

* Difficulty finding relevant content (10/10 respondents)
* Confusing navigation (9/10 respondents)
* Difficulty using the calendar or 'What's On' pages (10/10 respondents)
* Poor mobile experience (5/10 respondents)
* Difficulty updating or managing website content (3/10 respondents)

### **Criticality of Improvements for Departmental Needs**

Improvements in **Content Discoverability, Content Accuracy, and Website Navigation** and **Graphic Design Improvements** were rated as **Extreme/High Criticality** by almost all respondents.

### **Specific Content Types, Features, or Workflows That Don't Meet Needs**

* **Collections Search Functionality:** Universally cited as insufficient, particularly the inability to search by geography, donor names, or specific artwork details, leading to manual workarounds.
* **Calendar Functionality:** Consistently reported as cumbersome and difficult to use for finding events, lectures, and daily programs.
* **Content Integration:** Lack of integrated content (e.g., related stories, videos, scholarly essays) directly on artwork or exhibition pages.
* **Workflow for Content Updates:** Unclear communication or slow turnaround for content updates from internal systems (TMS) to the live website.

### **Ranked Features and Improvements**

Among the most helpful features/improvements, **Improved website search** (Avg. 4.1), **Better collections information offerings** (Avg. 2.8) consistently ranked as top priorities. Other important features included multilingual content, more dynamic / interactive content, streamlined ticketing, improved analytics, and better internal content management tools.

### **How improvements would support department goals**

Improvements would primarily help departments:

* **Increased Collection Visibility & Accessibility:** Robust, easy-to-navigate collection pages and improved search functions will make the museum's permanent collections and related content (exhibitions, programs, articles) more discoverable to a broader audience, including researchers and the general public.
* **Streamlined Workflows & Research:** Enhanced search capabilities, including the ability to search by geography, donor name, and specific object details, will significantly reduce time spent on inquiries, facilitate curatorial work, and support external research, fulfilling the museum's educational mission.
* **Richer Storytelling & Global Reach:** Centralizing all related content (videos, audio, stories, essays) on artwork landing pages and offering content in multiple languages will enable more cohesive narratives and promote the museum's outstanding collections globally.

### **Workarounds & Vision for Success**

Departments frequently use internal databases (TMS, eMuseum) and manual processes (e.g., Google searches for external sharing) to compensate for website limitations. "Success" for website improvements largely revolves around **users being able to easily find and interact with centralized collection information, educational resources, and events**, with clearer navigation and more efficient internal workflows for content management. Many expressed a desire for the website to be a truly central, dynamic, and integrated hub for all museum content.

### **Departmental Goals & Opportunities**

The top website improvement goals are to **Improve overall user satisfaction** (Avg. 2.2), **Streamline internal departmental workflows** (Avg. 2.9), and **Enhance website accessibility for all users** (Avg. 2.9). Other important goals include increasing website traffic and online engagement.

New features desired include zoomable images, better search filters, and more integrated content. Opportunities for revenue include:

* **Integrate E-commerce Contextually:** Feature shop items (books, prints, merchandise) and "click to buy" buttons for related publications directly within relevant content and exhibition pages.
